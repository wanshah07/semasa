Complete Motion1. # Video edit specification — Viral Talking Head

_Compiled by CES — Claude Editing Styles (ces.wancraft.my) from a 9-style recipe. CES is an independent tool, not affiliated with Anthropic._

## A. Role and objective

You are an experienced motion designer and video editor working inside Claude Code. Your task is to edit the supplied footage into a finished 30-second 9:16 video.

Apply the 9 editing techniques below **in the exact order and at the timings given**. Sequential beats, overlays, captions, audio treatments and global looks are listed separately on purpose: do not flatten them into one linear sequence, and do not re-order them.

The expected result is an editable project plus a rendered MP4 that you have actually produced and verified.

## C. Editing principles

- Preserve the original meaning of what is said. Do not reorder statements in a way that changes their meaning.
- Keep spoken dialogue intact unless an instruction explicitly asks for trimming; cut only dead air, false starts and long pauses (> 0.6 s).
- Never invent visuals, proof, statistics, quotes, comments, screenshots or claims. Everything on screen must come from the supplied assets, the transcript or the parameters below.
- Preserve legibility: on-screen text stays within the safe zone, at ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, and long enough to read (~3 words per second).
- Effects serve the story. When an effect would obscure the speaker's face or a key visual, scale it back.
- Keep motion physically coherent: consistent easing language, no unmotivated jumps, no more than one dominant motion per beat.
- Respect the platform safe zones for UI overlays (captions and text must clear the top status area and the bottom/right interaction rails).
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies. Prefer what is already installed; add packages only when they are needed for a listed technique.

### 01 · H01 Headline Slam — Primary sequence, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:00.00 → 00:01.80 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V1 (main picture)
- **What it does:** An oversized headline lands in the opening second with a controlled scale overshoot and settles above the speaker.
- **Parameters:** Headline text: (not set); Duration: 1.8 s; Scale: 2.2x; Easing: Back out (overshoot); Text size: 11%; Text position: Upper third; Text colour: #F4F2EB; Accent colour: #D7FF5F; Impact micro-shake: on
- **Steps:**
  1. Create a headline overlay layer above the source footage from 00:00.00 to 00:01.80 (1080×1920 @ 30fps).
  2. Headline copy: derive a 2–5 word promise strictly from the speaker's first sentence in the transcript (English). Never introduce a claim the speaker does not make.
  3. Typeset in Space Grotesk (bold/black weight), cap height ≈ 11% of frame width, colour #F4F2EB; render the single most important word in #D7FF5F. Break into at most two balanced lines.
  4. Entrance: scale from 2.2x to 1.0 over ~13 frames using a back-out curve with slight overshoot (cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)); opacity 0→1 within the first 3 frames; add 1–2 frames of directional motion blur on the way in.
  5. On the landing frame apply a 3–4 frame positional shake (≤ 1.2% of frame width, decaying) to the headline layer only — never to the face.
  6. Place in the upper third of the frame. Keep the speaker's eyes and mouth unobstructed and respect the platform safe zone.
  7. Hold for the remainder of 1.8s, then exit over 6 frames (opacity → 0, scale → 0.96) as the first sentence continues.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Brand fonts
- **Fallback:** If no transcript is available, ask for the headline text or transcribe the first 5 seconds locally before writing it. If brand fonts are missing, use Inter Black / Space Grotesk Bold.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Opens the sequence. Leads into P01 Screenshot Punch. While active, these also run: M01 Snap Zoom (visual overlay).

### 02 · M01 Snap Zoom — Visual overlay, single beat

- **When:** ≈ 00:00.80 (1.00 s) (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** The frame punches in on the speaker's face in a few frames exactly on a stressed word or hit, holds, then releases.
- **Parameters:** Scale: 1.25x; Zoom-in length: 5 frames; Hold: 0.8 s; Release: Ease back out; Beat source: Stressed word (transcript timing); Beat timecode: (not set); Easing: Expo out (snappy); Motion blur on the move: on
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply a snap zoom to the source video layer at 00:00.80 (window ends 00:01.80). Beat: use word-level transcript timing — place the effect on the first frame of the stressed word nearest 00:00.80. If only sentence timing exists, transcribe locally with word timestamps (e.g. Whisper word_timestamps=True) first.
  2. Scale the footage from 1.00 to 1.25x over 5 frames (167ms at 30fps) using a fast expo-out curve (cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)). Set the transform origin on the speaker's face (eye-line centre), not the frame centre — in Remotion: transformOrigin at the face box, scale = interpolate(frame, [beat, beat + 5], [1, 1.25], { extrapolateLeft: "clamp", extrapolateRight: "clamp", easing }).
  3. Add motion blur during the 5 zoom frames only: wrap the layer in @remotion/motion-blur <CameraMotionBlur shutterAngle={180} samples={8}> (or in FFmpeg, tmix=frames=3 on those frames). No blur on the held frames.
  4. Hold the 1.25x framing for 0.8s (24 frames). Then ease back to 1.00x over 10 frames with a smooth ease-in-out.
  5. Resolution headroom: probe the source (ffprobe -show_streams). The crop must keep ≥ 864×1536 real source pixels for a 1.25x zoom into a 1080×1920 output. If the source cannot supply that, cap the zoom at sourceWidth ÷ 1080 × 1.1 and report it — never upscale into visible softness.
  6. Keep eyes inside the upper third after the zoom and check that the face does not hit the platform UI safe zone. Use at most one snap zoom per ~4 seconds so it keeps its meaning.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Music track
- **Fallback:** If the source lacks resolution headroom, reduce the zoom to what the pixels allow (often 1.10–1.15x for 1080p into 1080p) and add a light unsharp mask instead of upscaling. Without word timing, ask the user to point to the beat.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Anchored to the H01 Headline Slam beat. While active, these also run: H01 Headline Slam (primary sequence).
- **Notes from the user:** First key claim.

### 03 · P01 Screenshot Punch — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:09.20 → 00:12.20 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** A real screenshot drops in over the speaker at a precise scale, lands with a soft overshoot and a deep shadow, holds long enough to read, then clears.
- **Parameters:** Screenshot file: (not set); Duration: 3 s; Entrance: Drop + punch (from above, overshoot); Screenshot width: 82%; Landing tilt: -2 deg; Shadow depth: 60%; Background treatment: Dim footage 45%; Easing: Back out (overshoot)
- **Steps:**
  1. Place screenshot from the project assets (the user's supplied screenshot; if none is present, stop and ask for it — do not substitute a mock-up) on an overlay layer above the source footage from 00:09.20 to 00:12.20 (1080×1920 @ 30fps). Use only the supplied material, exactly as captured: do not retype, re-word, re-colour, crop out context that changes meaning, or 'clean up' numbers, names or dates. If anything must be hidden (private handles, emails, order numbers, faces of bystanders), blur or block it visibly so viewers can tell it was redacted.
  2. Scale it to 82% of frame width (886px), preserving its aspect ratio; if it would exceed 70% of frame height, fit by height instead. Never upscale beyond 200% of its native pixels — if it would, show it smaller rather than blurry. Round corners by ≈ 2.5% of its width.
  3. Entrance over 14 frames: enter from 12% of frame height above its rest position at scale 1.12 and land at scale 1.0, using a back-out curve with slight overshoot (cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1)), while rotating from -5.0° to a resting tilt of -2°. Opacity reaches 1 within the first 3 frames.
  4. Shadow: two stacked drop shadows — a tight contact shadow (y offset 0.6% of frame width, blur 1.2%, 31% black) and a soft ambient one (y offset 3%, blur 8%, 37% black). Add a 1px 10% white inner stroke so dark screenshots separate from dark footage.
  5. Dim the underlying footage to 55% brightness over 9 frames as the screenshot enters, restoring it on exit.
  6. Land the punch on the exact word in the transcript where the speaker references the screenshot. Hold it fully still for at least 2.3s — long enough to read every word that matters (≈ 3 words per second); extend the range rather than cutting it short.
  7. Exit over 8 frames (scale 1.0 → 0.96, opacity → 0, ease-in). Verify on a phone-sized render that the key text in the screenshot is legible at 82% width.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING), Screenshots (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Sound effects
- **Fallback:** If no screenshot is supplied, do not recreate, mock up or 'illustrate' the evidence — omit this beat and list it as a missing asset, or ask the user for the capture. If the screenshot is too low-resolution to read, show it smaller with a crop around the relevant area (keeping enough context to stay honest) and flag it.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Follows H01 Headline Slam. Leads into V10 Statement Reveal. While active, these also run: M01 Snap Zoom (visual overlay).

### 05 · V10 Statement Reveal — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:19.60 → 00:22.60 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** A strong sentence appears in stages — phrase by phrase in muted type — and the final key words land last in full colour with a heavier weight.
- **Parameters:** Statement: (not set); Staging: Phrase chunks; Emphasis words: (not set); Reveal pace: 3; Backdrop: Dimmed footage; Text size: 8%; Accent colour: #D7FF5F
- **Steps:**
  1. No statement is set. Use the speaker's exact sentence from the transcript at this moment; emphasise its final 1–3 words unless the user specifies others.
  2. Dim the footage 55% from 00:19.60 to 00:22.60. Typeset in Space Grotesk at ≈ 8% of frame width, left-aligned in the middle third, max 4 lines.
  3. Reveal in natural phrase chunks (2–4 words, split at punctuation/breath) at ≈ 3 words/second (2.7s total), ideally keyed to the word timestamps; each chunk fades and rises 2% over 8 frames with a fast expo-out curve (cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)).
  4. Earlier chunks settle to 55% opacity in regular weight; the emphasis words arrive last in bold and #D7FF5F, scaling 1.08 → 1.0 over 6 frames.

### 06 · S01 Impact Hit — Audio, single beat

- **When:** ≈ 00:21.10 (0.80 s) (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** A2+ (under the original voice on A1)
- **What it does:** A short, punchy impact sound lands on the exact frame a major graphic or reveal hits the screen, mixed under the voice.
- **Parameters:** Impact sound file: (not set); Level: -12 dB; Sync offset (ms): -20; Character: Punchy hit; Tail fade (ms): 250; Dialogue-safe music dip: -3 dB
- **Steps:**
  1. ASSET REQUEST: no sound effect was supplied. Ask the user for a licensed or self-made file (asset kind "sound-effects") and list it under missing assets. Never download copyrighted audio. Until it arrives: leave the moment silent (optional silence-based fallback) rather than synthesising a fake impact.
  2. Place the impact at 00:21.10 so its transient peak sits -20ms (-1 frames at 30fps) relative to the frame where the graphic lands. Align on the waveform peak, not the file start — trim any pre-roll silence first.
  3. Level: peak -12 dBFS on the SFX bus (≈ -18 to -16 LUFS short-term), always at least 4 dB under the dialogue's short-term loudness when they overlap. High-pass at 40 Hz; a gentle +2 dB shelf at 3 kHz keeps the attack audible on phone speakers.
  4. Fade the tail out over 250ms with an exponential curve; cut anything after the fade. Dip the music bed by -3 dB for the length of the hit (10ms attack, 200ms release).
  5. Remotion: <Sequence from={hitFrame + (-20 / 1000) * fps}><Audio src={staticFile("…")} volume={0.25} /></Sequence>; FFmpeg: adelay + volume=-12dB + afade=t=out, mixed with amix=normalize=0.
  6. Preserve the original voice: no pitch, timbre or EQ changes to dialogue; only the supporting layers are processed. Master bus: dialogue at -16 to -14 LUFS integrated for social delivery, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP (ffmpeg loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1:LRA=11, two-pass), and check the result with ebur128.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING), Sound effects (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Music track
- **Fallback:** If no licensed impact sound is supplied, request one and render the moment without it (silence-based fallback) — or use the S05 silence drop to create emphasis with no new audio.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Anchored to the V10 Statement Reveal beat. While active, these also run: V10 Statement Reveal (visual overlay).

### 07 · S10 Outro Audio Resolve — Audio, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:27.00 → 00:30.00 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** A2+ (under the original voice on A1)
- **What it does:** The music is edited to land on its final chord as the last frame arrives, then rings out in a controlled tail to silence.
- **Parameters:** Music track: (not set); Ending: Back-time to the song's real ending; Tail length: 3 s; Fade shape: Exponential (natural decay); Gap after last word: 0.5 s; Silence at end: 12 frames
- **Steps:**
  1. ASSET REQUEST: no music track was supplied. Ask the user for a licensed or self-made file (asset kind "music") and list it under missing assets. Never download copyrighted audio. Until it arrives: end on the natural room tone of the last shot with a 1s fade — never substitute a downloaded track.
  2. Back-time the track: align the song's own final chord so it hits 0.5s after the last spoken word, then edit earlier in the song (on a bar line, matching beats) so the music reaches that ending naturally.
  3. Shape the tail over 3.0s (90 frames) with an exponential fade (FFmpeg afade=t=out:curve=exp) so it reaches silence by 00:30.00 minus 12 frames.
  4. End with 12 frames (400ms) of digital silence before the last video frame so no platform clips the tail; the picture end card stays up through the tail.
  5. Keep the music under any CTA voice-over by at least 8 dB. Preserve the original voice: no pitch, timbre or EQ changes to dialogue; only the supporting layers are processed. - **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING), Music track (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Sound effects
- **Fallback:** No music supplied: request a licensed or self-owned track, or close on natural room tone with a gentle 1s fade. Never pull music from the internet.

## E. Global treatments

Apply global treatments after the edit is assembled, in this order: base grade → stylised look → texture → caption layer rules → final audio mix.

### F01 Clean Creator Grade — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Neutralises white balance, places skin at a healthy exposure, adds a gentle S-curve and a touch of saturation so every shot looks true-to-life and consistent.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 100%; White balance shift (K, + warmer): 0; Exposure trim: 0; Contrast: 8%; Saturation: 105%; Highlight roll-off: Soft (peak ≈ 94 IRE); Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Clean Creator grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1080×1920 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation. Pipeline order: source footage → primary balance → this grade → captions, titles, logos and UI graphics composited on top. Do not run brand graphics through the grade; #D7FF5F and the caption whites must render exactly as specified. Screen recordings and supplied screenshots get at most 30% of the grade strength so text and interface colours stay faithful.
  2. Primary balance per shot first: sample a neutral reference (white wall, shirt, paper) and trim colorbalance midtones until R≈G≈B on it; set exposure so the brightest skin sits at 65–72 IRE and the face average at 55–65 IRE; black point at 1–2 IRE (lift −1%, never crush shadow detail); gamma neutral; gain only until highlights reach the roll-off. Keep the result neutral (no Kelvin shift).
  3. Look (strength 100%): contrast +8% as a gentle S-curve pivoting at 50%, saturation 105% applied to greens, cyans, blues and magentas only (reds/yellows untouched so skin stays put), highlight roll-off: soft shoulder starting at 80% input, peak whites ≈ 94 IRE. FFmpeg chain: -vf "eq=contrast=1.08:saturation=1:gamma=1,curves=master='0/0.012 0.5/0.5 0.8/0.78 1/0.94',huesaturation=saturation=0.05:colors=g+c+b+m".
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.7[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "brightness(1) contrast(1.08) saturate(1.05)" }}>. CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. Whites must peak ≤ 94 IRE, blacks ≥ 1 IRE, the waveform should show no flat-topped clipping, and the vectorscope skin trace must sit on the skin line. Shots must match each other within ±2 IRE in skin exposure and ±3° in skin hue; fix outliers with a per-shot exposure/white-balance trim before the look, never by changing the look itself. Export at 1080×1920, 30fps, 10-bit or high-bitrate 8-bit with -pix_fmt yuv420p to avoid banding in gradients and skies.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: LUT file
- **Fallback:** If shots were filmed under mixed lighting that cannot be neutralised with one balance, correct each shot's white balance individually first, then apply this grade uniformly. If the footage is already well balanced Rec.709, apply only the contrast/saturation trims at 50% strength.

### C01 Karaoke Follow — Caption layer, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Captions layer (topmost text)
- **What it does:** Short caption pages sit over the speaker and each word fills with the accent colour at the exact moment it is spoken.
- **Parameters:** Caption typeface: Bold sans (Space Grotesk / Inter Black); Text size: 7.5%; Highlight behaviour: Progressive fill (colour wipes through the word); Words on screen: 5; Spoken-word colour: #D7FF5F; Upcoming-word colour: #F4F2EB; Safe-zone offset: 18%; All caps: on
- **Steps:**
  1. Add a karaoke caption layer across every spoken section of the video on its own track above all footage and graphics (1080×1920 @ 30fps).
  2. Timing source: use the supplied transcript's word-level timestamps for the English dialogue. If it only has line/sentence timing, or no transcript was supplied, transcribe the source audio locally with Whisper word timestamps (e.g. `whisper source.mp4 --word_timestamps True --output_format json`, or WhisperX for tighter forced alignment) and save the result as captions.json. Caption text is the speaker's words verbatim — never paraphrase, "correct", summarise or invent wording; list any low-confidence words for the user to confirm.
  3. Paging: group words into pages of at most 5 words and 30 characters per line (one line; two only when a page would exceed it), breaking at phrase boundaries — never between a number and its unit or inside a name. A page appears 2 frames before its first word and is replaced (hard cut, no fade) when the next page's first word starts; hold the last page at most 18 frames into silence, then clear.
  4. Highlight: every word starts in #F4F2EB and fills with #D7FF5F from left to right over its own spoken duration (render a #D7FF5F copy of the word clipped by an inset that tracks (frame − startFrame) / (endFrame − startFrame)), so the fill follows the syllables. Spoken words stay #D7FF5F; nothing scales or moves.
  5. Typeset in Space Grotesk at Black/ExtraBold weight (fallback: Inter Black), cap height ≈ 7.5% of frame width (≈ 81px), ALL CAPS with +2% tracking, with a 0.12em dark stroke or a 30% black drop shadow so both colours hold 4.5:1 contrast on bright footage.
  6. Placement: anchor the caption block's bottom edge 18% of frame height (346px at 1080×1920) above the bottom of the frame, horizontally centered with ≥ 8% side margins, so it stays clear of the TikTok / Reels / Shorts interface (description text and action buttons occupy roughly the bottom 20% and the right-hand 12% of the frame). Never cover the speaker's eyes or mouth; if the face sits unusually low in a shot, lift the block above it for that shot only.
  7. Render with Remotion: a <KaraokeCaptions /> component reads captions.json ([{ text, startMs, endMs }] grouped into pages) and derives the active word from useCurrentFrame() / 30. Verify by scrubbing three random sentences — the fill must start within ±1 frame of each audible word onset.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Brand fonts
- **Fallback:** If no transcript is supplied and local transcription is not possible, ask the user for an SRT/VTT. With only line-level timing, render whole-line captions without word highlights rather than spacing fake word times evenly — invented timing visibly drifts out of sync.

## F. Implementation guidance

- Start by inspecting every input: run `ffprobe -v error -show_streams -show_format <file>` on each video/audio asset and report resolution, frame rate (constant vs variable), duration, audio channels and codec. Stop and ask if a required file is missing or unreadable.
- Check what is available before choosing tools: Node.js version, FFmpeg/ffprobe, and whether a Remotion project already exists in the workspace.
- Use **Remotion** (React-based video) for motion graphics, captions, layouts and overlays. Create one composition at the configured width, height, fps and duration; keep each technique in its own component and drive timing with `useCurrentFrame()`, `interpolate()` and `spring()`.

### F03 Cool Technology — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Shifts whites neutral-to-cool, deepens blues, tightens contrast and keeps screen glows and LED accents controlled — a precise, modern tech finish with natural skin.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 90%; Cool shift (K): -500; Tint (− green / + magenta): -2; Contrast: 14%; Saturation: 92%; Electronic accent colour: #38D6FF; Accent rim glow: on; Protect skin tones: on
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Cool Technology grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1920×1080 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation. Pipeline order: source footage → primary balance → this grade → captions, titles, logos and UI graphics composited on top. Do not run brand graphics through the grade; #D7FF5F and the caption whites must render exactly as specified. Screen recordings and supplied screenshots get at most 30% of the grade strength so text and interface colours stay faithful.
  2. Balance each shot neutral, then cool the image by ≈500 K (colortemperature=temperature=7000:mix=0.9:pl=1) and apply tint -2 (colorbalance midtones gm=0.02). Lift −2% with a slight blue offset in shadows (colorbalance bs=0.05:rs=-0.03), gamma 0, gain held so whites read neutral-cool (#F2F6FF) at ≤ 96 IRE.
  3. Contrast +13% (curves=master='0/0.01 0.25/0.21 0.5/0.5 0.75/0.79 1/0.96'), overall saturation 93%, then boost only blues/cyans: huesaturation=saturation=0.11:colors=c+b. FFmpeg chain: -vf "colortemperature=temperature=7000:mix=0.9:pl=1,colorbalance=rs=-0.03:bs=0.05:gm=0.02:pl=1,eq=contrast=1.126:saturation=0.928,curves=master='0/0.01 0.25/0.21 0.5/0.5 0.75/0.79 1/0.96',huesaturation=saturation=0.11:colors=c+b".
  4. Electronic accents: keep screens, LEDs and neon in the #38D6FF family saturated but below 98% on every channel. Reinforce existing accent light with an edge glow only where it already exists in the shot — a soft #38D6FF rim (blurred subject-edge mask or a screen-blended radial at 15–25% opacity) on the side facing the light source. Never add glow to shots without a visible source.
  5. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.85[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "contrast(1.126) saturate(0.928) hue-rotate(-1deg)" }}>, then add <AbsoluteFill style={{ background: "#3D6DFF", mixBlendMode: "soft-light", opacity: 0.18 }} /> (with a CSS mask-image radial hole over faces when skin protection is on). CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. RGB parade: highlights with blue ≥ green ≥ red by 2–4%; no channel flat-topping on screens or LEDs; skin trace still on the skin line. Shots must match each other within ±2 IRE in skin exposure and ±3° in skin hue; fix outliers with a per-shot exposure/white-balance trim before the look, never by changing the look itself. Export at 1920×1080, 30fps, 10-bit or high-bitrate 8-bit with -pix_fmt yuv420p to avoid banding in gradients and skies.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: LUT file, Screen recording
- **Fallback:** If the footage was shot under warm tungsten light and the cool shift turns skin grey even with protection, halve the Kelvin shift and push the coolness into the shadows only (colorbalance shadows) instead of the whole image.

## C. Editing principles

- Preserve the original meaning of what is said. Do not reorder statements in a way that changes their meaning.
- Keep spoken dialogue intact unless an instruction explicitly asks for trimming; cut only dead air, false starts and long pauses (> 0.6 s).
- Never invent visuals, proof, statistics, quotes, comments, screenshots or claims. Everything on screen must come from the supplied assets, the transcript or the parameters below.
- Preserve legibility: on-screen text stays within the safe zone, at ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, and long enough to read (~3 words per second).
- Effects serve the story. When an effect would obscure the speaker's face or a key visual, scale it back.
- Keep motion physically coherent: consistent easing language, no unmotivated jumps, no more than one dominant motion per beat.
- Respect the platform safe zones for UI overlays (captions and text must clear the top status area and the bottom/right interaction rails).
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies. Prefer what is already installed; add packages only when they are needed for a listed technique.

### 01 · H10 Cinematic Cold Open — Primary sequence, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:00.00 → 00:03.50 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V1 (main picture)
- **What it does:** Opens on a deliberate pause — ambient detail, letterboxed framing and elegant title type — before a dramatic transition.
- **Parameters:** Title: (not set); Opening pause: 1.2 s; Ambient detail: Supplied B-roll detail shot; Transition into story: Fade through black; Letterbox bars: on; Duration: 3.5 s
- **Steps:**

  2. Apply letterbox bars to a 2.39:1 picture area inside the 16:9 frame (bars are pure black, no feathering).
  3. Fade in the title (project title from the profile, or ask) in an editorial serif (or Space Grotesk if the brand requires), 24–40 tracking, small size (~4% of frame width), over 18 frames; hold 1.2s; fade out over 12 frames.

### 02 · L08 Cinematic Letterbox — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:17.67 → 00:25.67 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** Black bars close in from top and bottom to a true 2.39:1, 2:1 or 1.85:1 band, cropping (never stretching) the shot into a cinematic frame.
- **Parameters:** Letterbox ratio: 2.39:1 (anamorphic scope); Reframing: Crop to the band (no scaling); Bar animation: 0.6 s; Band position: 0%; Bar colour: #000000; Use bars for titles/captions: on; Easing: Ease in-out (smooth)
- **Steps:**
  1. Between 00:17.67 and 00:25.67, letterbox the 1920×1080 frame to 2.39:1: visible band 1920×803px, bars 139px each (top bar 139px, bottom bar 139px with the 0% band offset) in #000000.
  2. Reframing: crop the source to the band without scaling; position the crop so the subject's eyes sit on the band's upper third (track per shot if the subject moves). Scale every source uniformly (object-fit: cover for crops, contain for fits) — never stretch footage, screenshots or recordings to a region's aspect ratio.
  3. Bars animate in from the frame edges over 18 frames using a smooth ease-in-out curve (cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)) at the start, and out the same way at the end; the crop moves in sync so the subject stays inside the closing band.
  4. Use the bars as typographic space: titles or captions for this section sit centred in the bottom bar (and labels in the top bar) in Inter, never overlapping the picture band. Text comes from the user's params or the transcript only.
  5. Implement in Remotion with two absolutely-positioned bars whose height is interpolated, over an <AbsoluteFill> crop; or in FFmpeg with crop=1920:803:0:(ih-803)/2+0,pad=1920:1080:0:(oh-ih)/2-0:color=0x000000.

### 03 · M03 Slow Cinematic Push — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:39.83 → 00:45.83 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** The shot creeps in by a few percent over several seconds toward the speaker's eyes, raising intensity without a visible move.
- **Parameters:** Scale: 1.08x; Duration: 6 s; Push toward: Speaker's eye-line; Lateral drift: 0.5%; Easing: Linear; Restart after each cut: on
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply a slow push-in to the source video layer at 00:39.83 (window ends 00:45.83), lasting 6.0s (180 frames).
  2. Scale from 1.000 to 1.080x across the whole range using linear interpolation, with transform origin at the speaker's eye-line (detect the face box once and keep the origin fixed). That is 1.33% per second — slow enough that it is felt rather than seen.
  3. Add a 0.5% (10px) lateral drift in one direction over the same range for a dolly feel; never reverse direction mid-shot.
  4. Render with sub-pixel precision (Remotion transforms are fine; in FFmpeg use zoompan with d=1 and a high-resolution intermediate, or scale=3840:-1 before zoompan) so the push does not shimmer or step.
  5. If the range contains cuts, restart the push from 1.000x on every new shot.
  6. Resolution headroom: probe the source (ffprobe -show_streams). The crop must keep ≥ 1778×1000 real source pixels for a 1.08x zoom into a 1920×1080 output. If the source cannot supply that, cap the zoom at sourceWidth ÷ 1920 × 1.1 and report it — never upscale into visible softness.

### 04 · M02 Whip Transition — Visual overlay, transition at a cut

- **When:** ≈ 00:45.66 (0.35 s) (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** Shot A whips out of frame in a streak of directional blur and shot B whips in from the same direction, hiding the cut mid-motion.
- **Parameters:** Direction: Left; Transition length: 10 frames; Motion blur strength: 70%; Shutter angle: 270 deg; Easing: Ease in-out (smooth); Settle overshoot: on
- **Steps:**
  1. At the cut boundary 00:45.66, build a 10-frame whip (333ms): 5 frames of shot A exiting and 5 frames of shot B entering, with the actual cut on the fastest frame.
  2. Shot A: translate left by ~1152px (60% of frame width) accelerating with an ease-in; shot B: start 1152px off the opposite side and decelerate to rest using a smooth ease-in-out curve (cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)). Both move in the SAME direction (left).
  3. Motion blur: horizontal only, shutter angle 270° — in Remotion use <CameraMotionBlur shutterAngle={270} samples={9}> around both layers; in FFmpeg approximate with a horizontal directional blur (e.g. dblur=angle=0:radius=18) peaking on the cut frame and falling to zero at both ends.
  4. Let shot B overshoot its resting position by ~2% of frame width, settling back within 4 frames.
  5. Overscale both shots to 1.04x during the whip so frame edges never reveal black; return to 1.00x within 3 frames after landing.
  6. Verify the cut does not split a spoken word: if needed, shift the boundary up to 4 frames to the nearest pause and keep dialogue audio continuous under the whip.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Second shot, B-roll footage, Sound effects
- **Fallback:** If both shots are static and very similar, a whip can feel unmotivated — use a simple hard cut or a mask swipe (M09) instead. If motion blur rendering is too slow, use a 3-frame tmix blend around the cut.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Anchored to the M03 Slow Cinematic Push beat. While active, these also run: M03 Slow Cinematic Push (visual overlay), S03 Tension Riser (audio). ⚠ Consider reducing the intensity of one of these camera moves.

### 05 · S02 Cinematic Whoosh — Audio, single beat

- **Layer / track:** A2+ (under the original voice on A1)
- **What it does:** A whoosh swells into a big transition and peaks on the cut, panned in the direction of the on-screen motion.
- **Parameters:** Whoosh sound file: (not set); Level: -16 dB; Align peak to: The cut frame; Whoosh length (ms): 450; Stereo movement: Follow the on-screen direction; High-pass filter (Hz): 150
- **Steps:**
  1. ASSET REQUEST: no sound effect was supplied. Ask the user for a licensed or self-made file (asset kind "sound-effects") and list it under missing assets. Never download copyrighted audio. - **Parameters:** Riser sound file: (not set); Rise length: 3 s; Start level: -36 dB; Peak level: -12 dB; Rise curve: Exponential (late surge); Ending: Hard cut on the reveal; Low-pass filter sweep: on

### F04 Cinematic Teal Shadows — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Pushes only the shadows and darker midtones toward teal, warms highlights slightly and qualifies skin out of the shift, giving a filmic teal-and-warm separation.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 85%; Shadow tint: #0F7C82; Shadow tint amount: 45%; Shadow range (luma pivot): 35%; Highlight warmth: 25%; Contrast: 12%; Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Cinematic Teal Shadows grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1920×1080 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation.
  2. Primary balance first (neutral whites, skin 55–70 IRE, black point 1–2 IRE). Then contrast +10% via curves=master='0/0.015 0.24/0.22 0.5/0.5 0.8/0.82 1/0.96' — a slightly deeper toe with a soft highlight shoulder. Highlights: warm by 21% — colorbalance rh=0.02:gh=0:bh=-0.02:pl=1 — so faces and practical lights separate from the teal shadows; keep overall saturation at 100–105% and do not boost orange.
  3. Shadow tint #0F7C82 at 38%: restrict it to luma below 35% (full tint at 0–21%, fading to zero by 35%). FFmpeg: colorbalance=rs=-0.08:gs=0:bs=0:pl=1 (shadow band only); in Remotion/compositing, use a luma-keyed copy (lumakey or a curves-derived matte below 35% luma) tinted #0F7C82 in soft-light/colour blend at 38% opacity.
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.9[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "contrast(1.102) saturate(1.04)" }}>, then add an <AbsoluteFill style={{ background: "linear-gradient(180deg, transparent 44%, #0F7C82)", mixBlendMode: "soft-light", opacity: 0.38, maskImage: radial-gradient over the face (transparent at the face, opaque elsewhere) }} />. CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. ### F08 Film Grain & Bloom — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Layers fine moving film grain over the graded image and lets only the brightest highlights bloom into a soft glow, while faces and detail stay sharp.
- **Parameters:** Grain amount: 30%; Grain size: Medium (16mm); Bloom threshold: 85%; Bloom radius: 24 px; Bloom strength: 35%; Red halation: off
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Film Grain & Bloom texture as one global adjustment across the entire 1920×1080 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation.
  2. Place this pass AFTER the base grade and BEFORE captions/graphics. Bloom: split the graded image, keep only luma above 85% (curves=all='0/0 0.85/0 1/1'), blur it with gblur=sigma=22 (≈ 43px radius at 1920px width), then screen it back at 35%: [g]split[a][b];[b]curves=all='0/0 0.85/0 1/1',gblur=sigma=22[bl];[a][bl]blend=all_mode=screen:all_opacity=0.35.
  3. Grain: medium (16mm-like, ~1.5px structure) at 30% — generate it fresh every frame (temporal, never a frozen overlay): noise=c0s=11:c0f=t+u (luma only; leave chroma clean) and soften the grain structure for the larger size by applying it to a separate noise plate: color=gray:s=1920x1080,noise=c0s=11:c0f=t+u,gblur=sigma=0.6 blended with blend=all_mode=overlay:all_opacity=0.4. Grain must be strongest in midtones and nearly invisible in pure blacks and clipped whites (overlay blending does this).
  4. Full FFmpeg graph (after the base grade): -filter_complex "[0:v]split[a][b];[b]curves=all='0/0 0.85/0 1/1',gblur=sigma=22[bl];[a][bl]blend=all_mode=screen:all_opacity=0.35,noise=c0s=11:c0f=t+u[out]" -map "[out]".

  6. Restraint: blooms must not swallow faces — skin must stay below the threshold (if a bright forehead blooms, raise the threshold by 3–5%). Text, UI recordings and screenshots composited later must stay grain-free and sharp.

- **Fallback:** If the final delivery platform recompresses heavily, reduce grain to 15–20% and use the fine size, otherwise it turns into blocky noise. Without visible highlights (flat overcast footage), skip the bloom and keep only grain.

## C. Editing principles

- Preserve the original meaning of what is said. Do not reorder statements in a way that changes their meaning.
- Keep spoken dialogue intact unless an instruction explicitly asks for trimming; cut only dead air, false starts and long pauses (> 0.6 s).
- Never invent visuals, proof, statistics, quotes, comments, screenshots or claims. Everything on screen must come from the supplied assets, the transcript or the parameters below.
- Preserve legibility: on-screen text stays within the safe zone, at ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, and long enough to read (~3 words per second).
- Effects serve the story. When an effect would obscure the speaker's face or a key visual, scale it back.
- Keep motion physically coherent: consistent easing language, no unmotivated jumps, no more than one dominant motion per beat.
- Respect the platform safe zones for UI overlays (captions and text must clear the top status area and the bottom/right interaction rails).
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies. Prefer what is already installed; add packages only when they are needed for a listed technique.

### F01 Clean Creator Grade — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Neutralises white balance, places skin at a healthy exposure, adds a gentle S-curve and a touch of saturation so every shot looks true-to-life and consistent.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 100%; White balance shift (K, + warmer): 0; Exposure trim: 0; Contrast: 8%; Saturation: 105%; Highlight roll-off: Soft (peak ≈ 94 IRE); Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Clean Creator grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1080×1920 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation. Pipeline order: source footage → primary balance → this grade → captions, titles, logos and UI graphics composited on top. Do not run brand graphics through the grade; #D7FF5F and the caption whites must render exactly as specified. Screen recordings and supplied screenshots get at most 30% of the grade strength so text and interface colours stay faithful.
  2. Primary balance per shot first: sample a neutral reference (white wall, shirt, paper) and trim colorbalance midtones until R≈G≈B on it; set exposure so the brightest skin sits at 65–72 IRE and the face average at 55–65 IRE; black point at 1–2 IRE (lift −1%, never crush shadow detail); gamma neutral; gain only until highlights reach the roll-off. Keep the result neutral (no Kelvin shift).
  3. Look (strength 100%): contrast +8% as a gentle S-curve pivoting at 50%, saturation 105% applied to greens, cyans, blues and magentas only (reds/yellows untouched so skin stays put), highlight roll-off: soft shoulder starting at 80% input, peak whites ≈ 94 IRE. FFmpeg chain: -vf "eq=contrast=1.08:saturation=1:gamma=1,curves=master='0/0.012 0.5/0.5 0.8/0.78 1/0.94',huesaturation=saturation=0.05:colors=g+c+b+m".
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.7[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.

Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "brightness(1) contrast(1.08) saturate(1.05)" }}>. CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.

Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. Whites must peak ≤ 94 IRE, blacks ≥ 1 IRE, the waveform should show no flat-topped clipping, and the vectorscope skin trace must sit on the skin line. Shots must match each other within ±2 IRE in skin exposure and ±3° in skin hue; fix outliers with a per-shot exposure/white-balance trim before the look, never by changing the look itself. Export at 1080×1920, 30fps, 10-bit or high-bitrate 8-bit with -pix_fmt yuv420p to avoid banding in gradients and skies.

- **Layer / track:** Whole composition

### F01 Clean Creator Grade — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Neutralises white balance, places skin at a healthy exposure, adds a gentle S-curve and a touch of saturation so every shot looks true-to-life and consistent.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 100%; White balance shift (K, + warmer): 0; Exposure trim: 0; Contrast: 8%; Saturation: 105%; Highlight roll-off: Soft (peak ≈ 94 IRE); Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Clean Creator grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1920×1080 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation. Pipeline order: source footage → primary balance → this grade → captions, titles, logos and UI graphics composited on top. Do not run brand graphics through the grade; #D7FF5F and the caption whites must render exactly as specified. Screen recordings and supplied screenshots get at most 30% of the grade strength so text and interface colours stay faithful.
  2. Primary balance per shot first: sample a neutral reference (white wall, shirt, paper) and trim colorbalance midtones until R≈G≈B on it; set exposure so the brightest skin sits at 65–72 IRE and the face average at 55–65 IRE; black point at 1–2 IRE (lift −1%, never crush shadow detail); gamma neutral; gain only until highlights reach the roll-off. Keep the result neutral (no Kelvin shift).
  3. Look (strength 100%): contrast +8% as a gentle S-curve pivoting at 50%, saturation 105% applied to greens, cyans, blues and magentas only (reds/yellows untouched so skin stays put), highlight roll-off: soft shoulder starting at 80% input, peak whites ≈ 94 IRE. FFmpeg chain: -vf "eq=contrast=1.08:saturation=1:gamma=1,curves=master='0/0.012 0.5/0.5 0.8/0.78 1/0.94',huesaturation=saturation=0.05:colors=g+c+b+m".
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.7[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "brightness(1) contrast(1.08) saturate(1.05)" }}>. CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. Whites must peak ≤ 94 IRE, blacks ≥ 1 IRE, the waveform should show no flat-topped clipping, and the vectorscope skin trace must sit on the skin line. Shots must match each other within ±2 IRE in skin exposure and ±3° in skin hue; fix outliers with a per-shot exposure/white-balance trim before the look, never by changing the look itself. Export at 1920×1080, 30fps, 10-bit or high-bitrate 8-bit with -pix_fmt yuv420p to avoid banding in gradients and skies.

### F01 Clean Creator Grade — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Neutralises white balance, places skin at a healthy exposure, adds a gentle S-curve and a touch of saturation so every shot looks true-to-life and consistent.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 100%; White balance shift (K, + warmer): 0; Exposure trim: 0; Contrast: 8%; Saturation: 105%; Highlight roll-off: Soft (peak ≈ 94 IRE); Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Clean Creator grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1920×1080 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation. Pipeline order: source footage → primary balance → this grade → captions, titles, logos and UI graphics composited on top. Do not run brand graphics through the grade; #D7FF5F and the caption whites must render exactly as specified. Screen recordings and supplied screenshots get at most 30% of the grade strength so text and interface colours stay faithful.
  2. Primary balance per shot first: sample a neutral reference (white wall, shirt, paper) and trim colorbalance midtones until R≈G≈B on it; set exposure so the brightest skin sits at 65–72 IRE and the face average at 55–65 IRE; black point at 1–2 IRE (lift −1%, never crush shadow detail); gamma neutral; gain only until highlights reach the roll-off. Keep the result neutral (no Kelvin shift).
  3. Look (strength 100%): contrast +8% as a gentle S-curve pivoting at 50%, saturation 105% applied to greens, cyans, blues and magentas only (reds/yellows untouched so skin stays put), highlight roll-off: soft shoulder starting at 80% input, peak whites ≈ 94 IRE. FFmpeg chain: -vf "eq=contrast=1.08:saturation=1:gamma=1,curves=master='0/0.012 0.5/0.5 0.8/0.78 1/0.94',huesaturation=saturation=0.05:colors=g+c+b+m".
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.7[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "brightness(1) contrast(1.08) saturate(1.05)" }}>. CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. ## C. Editing principles

- Preserve the original meaning of what is said. Do not reorder statements in a way that changes their meaning.
- Keep spoken dialogue intact unless an instruction explicitly asks for trimming; cut only dead air, false starts and long pauses (> 0.6 s).
- Never invent visuals, proof, statistics, quotes, comments, screenshots or claims. Everything on screen must come from the supplied assets, the transcript or the parameters below.
- Preserve legibility: on-screen text stays within the safe zone, at ≥ 4.5:1 contrast, and long enough to read (~3 words per second).
- Effects serve the story. When an effect would obscure the speaker's face or a key visual, scale it back.
- Keep motion physically coherent: consistent easing language, no unmotivated jumps, no more than one dominant motion per beat.
- Respect the platform safe zones for UI overlays (captions and text must clear the top status area and the bottom/right interaction rails).
- Avoid unnecessary dependencies. Prefer what is already installed; add packages only when they are needed for a listed technique.

### 01 · H02 Pattern Interrupt — Primary sequence, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:00.00 → 00:01.00 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V1 (main picture)
- **What it does:** Breaks a conventional opening with a sharp contrast — a freeze, an inverted flash, a sudden reframe or a disruptive title.
- **Parameters:** Interrupt type: Freeze + inverted flash; Interrupt word: "WAIT."; Duration: 1 s; Intensity: 70%; Accent colour: #D7FF5F
- **Steps:**
  1. Let the opening play normally for 8–15 frames (interrupt at ≈ 00:00.35), then break the pattern.
  2. At the interrupt point hold the current frame (freeze) and flash a colour-inverted copy for 2 frames at 75% opacity, then return to the frozen normal frame.
  3. Overlay the interrupt word "WAIT." in Space Grotesk black weight, accent #D7FF5F, centered, for the interrupt duration (1.0s total).
  4. Resume normal playback on the next spoken syllable; the interrupt must never cut a word in half.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Sound effects
- **Fallback:** If the opening has no natural pause, place the interrupt on the first breath between sentences. Inverted flashes are skipped when the user requests photosensitivity-safe output.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Opens the sequence. Leads into V02 Number Burst. ⚠ Two headline treatments appear at once.

### 02 · V02 Number Burst — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:01.63 → 00:04.13 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** Numbers or ranking positions from the script burst onto the frame one after another with a staggered scale pop and radiating accent sparks.
- **Parameters:** Numbers / rankings (one per line): (not set); Mode: Ranking positions (#3 → #1); Stagger: 0.35 s; Burst accent: Radiating sparks; Text size: 14%; Hold after last: 1 s; Accent colour: #D7FF5F
- **Steps:**
  1. No values are set. Take every number or ranking position verbatim from the transcript (the ranking order the speaker announces). Never invent, round or re-order numbers; if the speaker gives none, ask the user.
  2. Each value: Space Grotesk black, cap height ≈ 14% of frame width, colour #D7FF5F, entering with scale 0 → 1.18 → 1.0 over 10 frames (a back-out curve with slight overshoot (cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1))) and a 3-frame opacity fade.
  3. Stagger entries by 0.35s (11 frames) — or, better, sync each to the word where the speaker says it — across 3 values from 00:01.63 to 00:04.13.
  4. On each landing, emit 8 short #D7FF5F strokes radiating from the number's center (length 4% → 0 of frame width, travelling 10% outward over 9 frames, ease-out) — purely decorative, no extra text.
  5. When a new value lands, shrink previous ones to 60% scale and 50% opacity (or move them into a row) so the newest is always dominant. Hold the final state 1.0s, then fade all over 8 frames.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Verified data, Sound effects
- **Fallback:** If the script contains no explicit numbers, skip this beat — do not create rankings or figures to fill it. Without SFX the visual pop works alone.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Follows H02 Pattern Interrupt. Leads into V04 List Cascade. ⚠ Two headline treatments appear at once.

### 03 · V04 List Cascade — Visual overlay, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:04.75 → 00:09.75 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** List items slide in one after another with numbered markers; the current item stays bright while earlier ones step back, keeping a clear hierarchy.
- **Parameters:** List items (one per line): (not set); Marker: Numbers; Enter from: Left; Gap between items: 0.8 s; Dim earlier items: on; Text size: 6%; Accent colour: #D7FF5F
- **Steps:**
  1. No items are set. Take the list items from the transcript, condensed to ≤ 6 words each using the speaker's own wording; do not add items they did not say.
  2. Layout from 00:04.75 to 00:09.75: left-aligned column at 10% from the left edge, starting in the upper third, row height ≈ 11.4% of frame width. Text in Inter semibold at 6% of frame width; numbers in Space Grotesk bold, #D7FF5F in a fixed-width gutter.
  3. Each item: marker pops (scale 0 → 1 over 6 frames), then the text slides in from the left (8% of frame width) with opacity 0 → 1 over 11 frames using a fast expo-out curve (cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)). Items start 0.8s apart, or on the word where each is spoken.
  4. When a new item lands, dim previous items to 45% opacity; the active item stays 100% with its marker in the accent colour.

- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Transcript with timing, Brand fonts

### 04 · X06 Magnetic Mosaic — Primary sequence, time range

- **When:** ≈ 00:10.38 → 00:14.38 (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V1 (main picture)
- **What it does:** Scattered media tiles drift and spin, then snap magnetically into a grid that assembles one larger supplied image or composition.
- **Parameters:** Grid: 4 × 4; Mosaic content: Slices of one supplied image; Scatter spread: 60%; Snap stagger: 0.05 s; Easing: Spring (physical); Close gaps on completion: on; Accent colour: #D7FF5F
- **Steps:**
  1. Cut to a mosaic cutaway from 00:10.38 to 00:14.38 with a 4×4 grid (16 tiles) occupying ≈ 86% of frame width, centred.
  2. Slice the single supplied image into 16 equal tiles (each tile keeps its exact region — background-position offsets in Remotion), so the completed grid reproduces the original image exactly.
  3. Scatter: start each tile at a random position within 60% of frame size around its slot, rotated ±40°, scaled 0.6–0.9, 0.3% blur, with a slow drift (seeded random so renders are deterministic).
  4. Snap: tiles fly to their slots in a wave from the centre outward, 0.05s (2 frames) apart, each over 14 frames with a physical spring (Remotion spring(): damping ≈ 12, stiffness ≈ 180, mass 1); rotation and blur reach 0 on landing.
  5. After the last tile lands, close the 1% gutters over 9 frames and remove corner radii so the tiles fuse into one image, with a single soft #D7FF5F edge glint.
  6. Hold the completed composition ≥ 1s before cutting back.
- **Assets:** Photos (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Screenshots, Brand logo
- **Fallback:** With a single low-resolution image, use a 3×3 grid so tiles are not shown enlarged. Without any images, ask for them — never generate mosaic content.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Follows V04 List Cascade. Closes the sequence. While active, these also run: M10 Stutter Motion (visual overlay).

### 05 · M10 Stutter Motion — Visual overlay, single beat

- **When:** ≈ 00:12.38 (0.50 s) (≈ derived — adjust to the nearest natural beat in the footage)
- **Layer / track:** V2+ (overlay above the picture)
- **What it does:** On a chosen beat the footage briefly repeats a few frames or drops into choppy stepped motion, like a deliberate skip, then runs normally again.
- **Parameters:** Stutter pattern: Repeat the last frames; Repeats: 3; Frames per group: 3 frames; Step rate: 8 fps; Beat source: Waveform peak / music hit; Audio: Keep original audio running
- **Steps:**
  1. Place the stutter at 00:12.38 (window ends 00:12.88). Beat: find the strongest transient in the dialogue/music waveform inside the instance window (e.g. ffmpeg -af "astats=metadata=1:reset=1" or librosa onset_detect) and snap the effect start to that frame. Report the frame you chose.
  2. Take the 3 frames that lead into the beat and play them 3 times in a row (9 frames, 300ms), then continue from the frame after the group. Remotion: sourceFrame = groupStart + ((frame − stutterStart) % 3) during the stutter; FFmpeg: loop=loop=2:size=3:start=<groupStartFrame>.
  3. Accent the first frame of each repeat with a 1-frame +6% brightness bump so the rhythm is visible.
  4. Keep the original audio running continuously underneath; trim the picture after the stutter so lip-sync is restored by the next word.
  5. Keep the whole stutter to about 300ms (never longer than ~600ms) and use it at most once per ~10 seconds.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: Music track, Transcript with timing
- **Fallback:** If there is no clear beat, use a single freeze beat (M04) or skip the effect; a random stutter reads as a playback error.
- **Interaction with adjacent styles:** Anchored to the X06 Magnetic Mosaic beat. While active, these also run: X06 Magnetic Mosaic (primary sequence).

### F06 Bold Pop Colour — Global treatment, whole video

- **When:** Applies to the whole video
- **Layer / track:** Whole composition
- **What it does:** Drives saturation and vibrance hard on props and backgrounds, pushes a chosen accent hue, adds punchy contrast — while guarding highlights and skin from clipping or going orange.
- **Parameters:** Grade strength (mix): 90%; Saturation: 145%; Vibrance: 35%; Contrast: 15%; Pop accent hue: #FFC21A; Highlight clip guard: on; Protect skin tones: on; Look LUT (.cube, optional): (not set)
- **Steps:**
  1. Apply the Bold Pop Colour grade as one global adjustment across the entire 1080×1920 @ 30fps timeline — every camera shot and b-roll clip gets identical settings; no shot is graded in isolation.
  2. Primary balance first (neutral whites, skin 60–70 IRE). Then contrast +14% (curves=master='0/0 0.25/0.2 0.5/0.5 0.75/0.8 1/0.98'), saturation 141% on non-skin hues (huesaturation=saturation=0.41:colors=g+c+b+m), and vibrance 32% (vibrance=intensity=0.32) so muted colours catch up with vivid ones.
  3. Accent push: qualify the hue family of #FFC21A (±15° around its hue, saturation > 25%) and raise its saturation a further 15% and luminance +3% — FFmpeg: huesaturation with colors set to the matching primary/secondary (e.g. y for yellow, m for pink) at saturation=0.15:lightness=0.03. Push only backgrounds and props; never the subject's face. Clip guard: after all boosts, soft-clip every channel at 98% (curves=all='0/0 0.9/0.9 1/0.98') and check the RGB parade — no channel may flat-top on saturated reds, neon or skies. Pull saturation back on that hue rather than accepting clipping.
  4. Protect skin: sample the median skin colour from a well-exposed cheek/forehead area (hex, e.g. 0xC08A6E — use the real sample) and build a hue-qualified matte around the ~20–30° skin hue range: [pre]colorkey=color=<skinHex>:similarity=0.16:blend=0.10,alphaextract,negate,gblur=sigma=4[skinMatte]; [balanced][skinMatte]alphamerge,colorchannelmixer=aa=0.9[skin]; [graded][skin]overlay=format=auto. This keeps faces on the vectorscope skin line (≈123°) while the look shifts everything else. Check the matte is soft-edged and does not catch wood, sand or orange props; tighten similarity if it does.
  5. No LUT supplied — build the look from the numeric controls below (they are the complete recipe). Never download or embed third-party LUTs; if the user later supplies a .cube file, apply it with lut3d=file=…:interp=tetrahedral before these trims.
  6. Remotion approximation (motion-graphics plates, previews, or when FFmpeg is not in the pipeline): wrap each footage <OffthreadVideo> in an <AbsoluteFill style={{ filter: "contrast(1.135) saturate(1.405) brightness(1.03)" }}>, then push the accent with a hue-qualified mask or a <AbsoluteFill style={{ background: "#FFC21A", mixBlendMode: "color", opacity: 0.25 }}/> restricted to the background matte (needs a subject mask, otherwise skip). CSS filters run in 8-bit display space, so for the final master prefer rendering the FFmpeg-graded footage and using that file in Remotion.
  7. Verify before export: grab one frame per shot (ffmpeg -ss <t> -i graded.mp4 -frames:v 1 shotN.png) and inspect ffmpeg -i shotN.png -vf "split[a][b];[a]waveform=mode=column:graticule=green:flags=numbers[w];[b]vectorscope=mode=color3:graticule=green[v];[w][v]hstack" scope.png. Vectorscope traces may reach ~75% of the radius on props but must stay inside the 100% target boxes; skin stays on the skin line with no orange push. Shots must match each other within ±2 IRE in skin exposure and ±3° in skin hue; fix outliers with a per-shot exposure/white-balance trim before the look, never by changing the look itself. Export at 1080×1920, 30fps, 10-bit or high-bitrate 8-bit with -pix_fmt yuv420p to avoid banding in gradients and skies.
- **Assets:** Source video (⚠ MISSING) · optional: LUT file
- **Fallback:** If the set is mostly beige or grey there is little colour to pop — apply at 50% strength and let the accent colour come from graphics instead. If skin cannot be keyed cleanly (warm walls), lower saturation to 125% rather than risk orange faces.

## Asset checklist

Gather these before (or while) running the edit:

- [ ] **Music track** — Rhythm Cuts, Stutter Motion, Beat Synchronization. A licensed or self-owned music file (WAV/MP3). CES never downloads copyrighted music.
- [ ] **Photos** — Magnetic Mosaic. Still photographs you own or have licensed, high resolution.
- [ ] **Source video** — All styles, Pattern Interrupt, Explosive Keywords, Number Burst, List Cascade, Rhythm Cuts, Stutter Motion, Beat Synchronization, Bold Pop Colour. The primary footage (e.g. talking-head recording). Highest available resolution, original audio intact.
- [ ] **B-roll footage** (optional) — Rhythm Cuts. Supplementary clips you own or have licensed.
- [ ] **Brand fonts** (optional) — Explosive Keywords, List Cascade. Font files or names of licensed typefaces for on-screen text.
- [ ] **Brand logo** (optional) — Magnetic Mosaic. SVG or transparent PNG logo for intros, outros and watermarks.
- [ ] **LUT file** (optional) — Bold Pop Colour. Optional .cube LUT for the grade. Without one, the grade is built from explicit colour operations.
- [ ] **Screenshots** (optional) — Magnetic Mosaic. Authentic PNG/JPG screenshots at native resolution. Never mocked-up evidence presented as real.
- [ ] **Sound effects** (optional) — Pattern Interrupt, Explosive Keywords, Number Burst. Licensed or self-made SFX files (impacts, whooshes, risers, ticks). Royalty-free libraries are fine if you hold the licence.
- [ ] **Transcript with timing** (optional) — Explosive Keywords, Number Burst, List Cascade, Rhythm Cuts, Stutter Motion, Beat Synchronization. An SRT/VTT/JSON transcript, ideally with word-level timestamps. Can be generated locally (e.g. Whisper) from the source video.
- [ ] **Verified data** (optional) — Number Burst. The real numbers to display, with their source. CES never invents statistics.
